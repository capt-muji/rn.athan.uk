## 1. The great circle vs rhumb line controversy

### The history, which is better documented than expected

- **The great circle has ~12 centuries of consensus among Muslim scientists.** David King, quoted in
  Abdali's *The Correct Qibla* (1997): "The qibla at any locality was defined as the direction to Mecca
  along the great-circle on the terrestrial sphere." The tradition: al-Khwarizmi, al-Battani, Habash
  al-Hasib, Ibn Yunus, al-Biruni, al-Tusi, al-Khalili, Ibn al-Shatir. [P]
- **The oldest North American great-circle mosque predates the controversy.** The Islamic Center of
  Washington DC (1953) faces **56°33′15″ east of true north**, calculated by the Egyptian Ministry of
  Works; doubters were checked by a National Geographic Society cartographer who confirmed it. [P]
- **1978, the flashpoint.** Abdali published *Prayer Schedules for North America*, giving a north/northeast
  qibla. "This conclusion was widely circulated and then accepted by the Muslim community, and mosques were
  subsequently reoriented as a result." [P]
- **1993, the counter-attack.** Riad Nachef and Samir Kadi of the **Association of Islamic Charitable
  Projects (AICP / Al-Ahbash)** published *The Substantiation of the People of Truth that the Direction of
  al-Qibla in the United States and Canada is to the Southeast*. Kadi, in the Orlando Sentinel (1994):
  *"This thing of the shortest way, none of the Islamic scholars speak of that... They all say the
  direction is the important thing."* Nachef to UPI: *"Unless he does this his prayer is invalid."* [P/J]
- **1997, the scientific rebuttal.** Abdali refuted the rhumb line geometrically: an infinitely tall tower
  over the Kaaba would appear on the horizon in the **great-circle** direction; a rhumb line is a spiral
  and cannot be a line of sight; **rhumb lines between two points are not even unique.** [P]
- **1998, al-Azhar's fatwa**, reproduced in Keller's *Port in a Storm* (2001): endorsed the great circle,
  and added "It is incumbent on all the Muslims to quit quarrelling... and to accept as compulsory what the
  ulema have decided in this matter." [P]
- **Where it settled:** "Eventually most North American Muslims accepted the north/northeast qibla with a
  minority following the east/southeast qibla." [P]

### Who ruled what

**For the great circle:** al-Azhar (1998), Darul Iftaa Chicago (a January **2025** fatwa, so the question is
still live), Askimam / Mufti Ebrahim Desai, IslamWeb (Qatar), Islamic Association of Raleigh,
moonsighting.com, and Sistani via a representative.

**For the southeast:** AICP / Al-Ahbash alone among organised bodies, plus Masjid Tucson (a Quranist
sect). AICP still publishes al-Harari's fatwa as of 2017.

**A notable Salafi middle position:** IslamQA fatwa 7535, relaying Ibn al-'Uthaymeen: *"the worshipper does
not have to pay attention to the curvature of the earth's surface... he can work out the direction of
Makkah by a straight line"* on a map, concluding the whole eastern sector is acceptable from California.
**This is neither great circle nor rhumb line: it is a broad-tolerance position.**

### Is it settled?

**Settled in practice, live in pockets.** AICP mosques still face southeast, their 1993 book is still
promoted, and fatwa services still answer the question as a live dispute in 2025.

**Could not verify:** a current census of SE-facing mosques (the "18 mosques" figure is 1994 advocacy
data), and any official published ISNA/FCNA resolution text.

## 2. How much precision is religiously required

### 'Ayn al-Ka'ba vs jihat al-Ka'ba

"'Ayn al-ka'ba" is facing the Kaaba head-on, such that a line from the eye would pass through it. "Given
that the Kaaba is less than 20 metres wide, this is virtually impossible from distant locations." From
Medina, 338 km away, **a 1° error misses the Kaaba by ~5.9 km.** "Most Islamic scholars consider that
jihat al-ka'ba is acceptable if the more precise ayn al-ka'ba cannot be ascertained." [P]

| School | Position |
| --- | --- |
| Hanafi | *jihat* suffices at distance. Al-Marghinani: "whoever is absent, his obligation is to face its direction, because obligation is according to ability" |
| Maliki | *jihat* suffices |
| Hanbali | *jihat* suffices |
| **Shafi'i** | **the outlier: *ayn* required even at distance, to the extent possible.** But a second opinion from al-Shafi'i himself holds *jihat* sufficient, and SeekersGuidance explicitly permits following it |

**Historical extreme:** some classical opinions accepted an **entire 90° quadrant**, and recorded Córdoba
mihrabs range 113° to 180°.

### The 45° tolerance: VERIFIED, with provenance

**It is real and widely issued, but it is a derived approximation rather than a canonical number.** The
underlying classical rule is that some part of the face must stay aligned with the Kaaba "or its air".

- **Egyptian Dar al-Ifta, Fatwa 6453 (2013):** "Scholars determined this to be 90 degrees i.e. **45 degrees
  from each direction**." [P, an official state muftiate]
- **Hanafi fatwa literature** (citing *Ahsan al-Fatawa*): "**Deviation beyond the 45 degrees mark
  invalidates the Salah.** Majority of the contemporary Hanafi scholars have adopted this opinion."
- **IslamQA 101449:** "If the deviation from the qiblah is less than 45 degrees, then your prayer is valid,
  because what you have to do is face the right direction, not face the Ka'bah or Makkah precisely."
- **Sistani (Twelver Shia):** the face "must not divert **a lot**" from qibla. **No numeric figure.**

**The agent's own caution, which matters:** one source traces the 45° phrasing to a later simplification,
noting "Hanafi Imams did not generally phrase it like that."

### Prayed in a direction later found wrong

**The deciding factor is diligence (ijtihad), not outcome.**

- **Diligent effort made:** prayer **valid, no repetition.** Ibn Qudamah reports this as the view of Malik,
  Abu Hanifa and al-Shafi'i. Ibn al-'Uthaymeen: "if he put effort into working it out... he does not have to
  repeat the prayer at all, **whether he got it right or not**."
- **Negligent** (did not ask, ignored the compass on their phone): **must repeat.**
- **IslamQA 483121 (2024) explicitly names the cell-phone compass** as "the most readily available" method,
  and treats ignoring it as negligence requiring repetition.

## 3. Facing through the earth

**The dichotomy is a misunderstanding.** Abdali: "the direction... of the straight tunnel between the two
points, is **also the great circle direction**." The chord lies vertically beneath the great-circle arc, in
the same vertical plane, so the same azimuth. **The only genuinely different alternative is the rhumb
line.** Nobody serious holds a through-the-earth position that differs from the great circle.

**The antipode** is at 21°25′24″S, 140°10′36″W, open Pacific near Tematagi Atoll. There every direction
reaches the Kaaba along an equal 10,008 km great circle, so any facing is correct. **No formal fatwa on
this was located**; it follows trivially from jihat. Engineering caveat from the agent: **display large
uncertainty near the antipode.**

## 4. Special cases

**The three-tier rule:** seeing the Kaaba means facing it exactly; in Mecca but not seeing it means facing
the Sacred Mosque; outside Mecca means facing the direction.

**On a plane:** nafl prayer may face any direction; obligatory prayer requires facing the qibla if able.
Abdali: *"Prayer is not supposed to be a gymnastic exercise... it's customary to start in the qibla
direction but then continue the prayer without worrying about possible changes in position."*

**In space:** Malaysia's Angkasa convened ~150 scholars and JAKIM published guidelines for Sheikh Muszaphar
Shukor's 2007 ISS flight. The qibla is determined "based on **what is possible**", with an explicit
fallback: 1) the Kaaba, 2) its projection, 3) the Earth, 4) wherever. On orbit the qibla can sweep ~180°
during a single prayer.

**When no compass is available**, the hierarchy is: follow the local mihrab, ask a trustworthy person, use
natural indicators (stars, sun), **use instruments if trustworthy** (the Saudi Standing Committee: "it may
be **obligatory** to act in accordance with it if a person cannot find any other evidence").

## 5. The sun method (rasd al-qibla)

| Event | Date and time | Verification |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sun over the Kaaba (1) | **27 or 28 May, 09:18 UTC** | Strong agreement across van Gent, Wikipedia, Abdali, Saudi Press Agency |
| Sun over the Kaaba (2) | **15 or 16 July, 09:27 UTC** | Strong. SPA gives 2026's instant to the second: 12:26:44 Makkah, 16 July |
| Sun over the antipode (1) | **12/13/14 January, ~21:29 UTC** | **Sources disagree on the day.** van Gent 12/13, Bashori 14, Pazmino 14 |
| Sun over the antipode (2) | **28 or 29 November, 21:09 UTC** | Strong, modulo the 28/29 variation |

**The agent's engineering ruling: compute the instant per-year in the app rather than hardcoding**, because
the day varies by year and sources disagree.

Classical pedigree: attested from al-Jaghmini (d. 1221) and al-Tusi (1201-1274), who specified **solar
longitude rather than a calendar date**, because the Islamic calendar is lunar.

Geographic limits: works only where the sun is above the horizon at that instant. The May event is 11
minutes **before sunrise in New York**, so unusable there; North America must use the January and November
antipodal events.

## 6. Sphere vs ellipsoid, per the jurists

Abdali holds the sphere sufficient ("does not affect accuracy in a practically significant way"); Pazmino
measures ~0.1° for New York; Helmi et al. 2024 measure 19.74″ to 20′26″ (up to ~0.34°). **Below the fiqh
tolerance either way; it matters only if the app claims arc-minute precision.**
