# What accuracy must the phone report before the compass is drawn? The Sunni sources, verified

Three rounds of research, twelve reports. The owner set the evidence standard himself:

🐋 "Many people on the internet can say anything... I don't want just some willy-nilly person online saying 10
degrees or 15 degrees and you accept it as truth. We need sources, validation. This cannot be fake data, must be
real data."

So every load-bearing claim below was **re-verified by the session itself at its primary source**, in Arabic where
the original is Arabic. Claims that failed verification are recorded as failures rather than dropped.

---

## 1. The headline: NO classical text in ANY school gives a number

Five researchers reading the actual texts (al-Hidayah, Radd al-Muhtar, Nihayat al-Muhtaj, al-Mughni,
al-Mudawwana, al-Majmu', Fath al-Bari) reached this independently. The classical rule is **qualitative**:
*inhiraf yasir* (slight, excused) against *inhiraf fahish* (major, invalidating), the test being whether any part
of the worshipper still aligns with the Kaaba or its airspace.

**Every degree figure in circulation is modern.** `10`, `15` and `22.5` were each marked NO AUTHENTIC SOURCE
FOUND. Anyone quoting a classical citation for a degree figure is mistaken.

## 2. The two contemporary poles, and why the app sits on the strict one

| | Precision pole | Convenience pole |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Figure | **3 degrees** | **±45 degrees** |
| Body | Malaysia National Fatwa Committee | Dar al-Ifta al-Misriyyah |
| Basis | modern technology, **Shafi'i-influenced** | historical factors, *maslahah*, **Hanafi-influenced** |
| Applies to | **new** determinations | mosques **already built** |

Source for both, read in full by this session: *Journal of Fatwa Management and Research* (USIM), vol. 31 no. 2,
2026, DOI 10.33102/jfatwa.vol.31no2.731.

**The lenient mosque rulings do not transfer to a device.** Every one is conditioned on an existing building.
Fatwa 12074's own title is "if the mosque is **already built**"; its question notes that correcting it would waste
large areas. The Perlis Fatwa Committee (38th sitting, 2018) splits it explicitly: existing mosques *"may remain"*
within 45 degrees, while mosques built now with modern instruments *"must strive for the exact direction"*. The
Palestinian Dar al-Ifta ruled a **42-degree** mosque `لا يجوز`, not permissible, with correction obligatory.

**A qibla app performs a NEW determination every time it opens.** It is on the inception side of that line.

## 3. The decisive text: the shari'a grades the METHOD, not the angle

**al-Nawawi, al-Majmu' 3/202-203**, fetched in Arabic and read by this session:

> «وإذا اجتهد وظن القبلة في جهة **بعلامة** صلى إليها، **ولا يكفي الظن بلا علامة بلا خلاف**»
>
> "When he exerts effort and thinks the qibla is in a direction **by an indicator**, he prays toward it; **bare
> conjecture without an indicator does not suffice, by consensus**."

And, the same passage:

> «ولو ترك القادر على الاجتهاد الاجتهاد... **لم تصح صلاته وإن صادف القبلة**»
>
> "If one capable of ijtihad abandons it... **his prayer is invalid even if he happened to hit the qibla**."

**This inverts the question.** A man facing the Kaaba exactly, with no legitimate means, has prayed INVALIDLY. A
man using a sound instrument who ends up 17 degrees off has prayed VALIDLY. The angle is not what is graded.

A phone compass is an `'alama`. The Kuwaiti Encyclopaedia of Fiqh (4/69) lists the magnetic needle by name among
the qibla's evidences.

## 4. An instrument may be relied on, and relying on it may be OBLIGATORY

**Permanent Committee fatwa 4254, vol. 6 pp. 318-320, signed by Ibn Baz**, read in the printed Arabic:

> «لكن إذا ثبت لدى أهل الخبرة الثقات من المسلمين أن جهازا أو آلة تضبط القبلة وتبينها عينا، أو جهة لم يمنع الشرع من
> الاستعانة بها... **بل قد يجب العمل بها** في معرفة القبلة إذا لم يجد من يريد الصلاة دليلا سواها»

The question that fatwa answers was about two qibla instruments **disagreeing by 17 degrees**. The Committee never
adopts a degree figure. It rules on whether the instrument is trustworthy.

**The classical conditions on an instrument** are qualitative too: al-Haskafi requires it be `صحيحة سليمة` (sound
and intact); Ibn Badran, `إن تكررت إصابته` (if its hits are repeated); and al-Ashraf's compass treatise (c. 1290)
labels every reading `بالتقريب`, approximately — a 13th-century instrument maker publishing his own uncertainty.

## 5. Refusing to show a direction is the one thing the sources condemn

**Islamweb 391144**, verified: a man delayed his prayer because he did not know the qibla.

> "Your not knowing the qibla **does not permit you to leave the prayer**... if you cannot even do ijtihad, you
> must pray toward **any** direction."

**The dark-night hadith**, graded *hasan* by al-Albani (*Irwa' al-Ghalil* 291): the Companions each guessed, and
the Prophet said **«قد أجزأت صلاتكم»**, your prayer counts.

There is no "refuse until certain" branch anywhere in this fiqh. A refusal screen pushes the worshipper from
lawful *zann* into *shakk*, which Muslim 571 commands be discarded.

## 6. The principle that resolves the conflict

The precision pole (3 degrees) is **unreachable** on real hardware indoors. The convenience pole (45 degrees) is
reachable but is the validity floor, which Imam Ahmad forbids aiming at:

**Imam Ahmad, in al-Istidhkar 7/220-221**, verified: asked whether prayer anywhere in the wide band is valid, he
answered «نعم **وينبغي أن يتحرى الوسط**» — "yes, **and he should seek the middle**."

The maxim that closes it, recorded by **al-Suyuti** in *al-Ashbah wa'l-Naza'ir* from **al-Subki**, and by **Ibn
Rajab al-Hanbali** in his *Qawa'id*:

> **«الميسور لا يسقط بالمعسور»** — the attainable is not forfeited because of the unattainable.

Al-Subki derives it from **Sahih al-Bukhari 7288**, verified at source in Arabic:

> «وَإِذَا أَمَرْتُكُمْ بِأَمْرٍ **فَأْتُوا مِنْهُ مَا اسْتَطَعْتُمْ**»
>
> "And when I command you to do something, **do of it what you can**."

**THE GOVERNING PRINCIPLE: take the best the means allow. Not the ideal that cannot be reached, and not the floor
that could be scraped.**

## 7. What the hardware can actually deliver

| Measurement | Value |
| --- | --- |
| The owner's iPhone XS, untethered, indoors, London | **~12.8 degrees**, `wantsCalibration false` |
| Lowest value in any public report found, any device | **10 degrees** |
| The 5-degree gate shipped in this prototype | **never fired once**; every draw came from the ceiling |
| `expo-location`'s own best band (`normalizeAccuracy`) | **0 to 20 degrees** |
| Every qibla and compass app examined on GitHub | gate at **15, 20 or 30**; none at or below 10 |

**Why 12.8 does not mean the compass is wrong.** `headingAccuracy` is a guarantee band, not a measured error:
Apple's own header calls it "the **maximum** deviation". A true error of 0 and a reported band of 12.8 are fully
consistent. Apple also states calibration "is able to filter out only those magnetic fields that **move with the
device**", which is why `wantsCalibration` reads false indoors: the field is in the room, and iOS knows no gesture
will remove it.

**And a tighter threshold does not buy correctness.** Two Apple Developer Forum reports, three years apart, show
`headingAccuracy` reporting 15 to 20 degrees while the heading was **over 100 degrees wrong**. The number measures
uncertainty; it does not detect wrongness.

## 8. The number, and what kind of claim it is

**`CERTAINTY_THRESHOLD_DEGREES = 15`.**

**No scholar said 15.** The sources give a principle, not a figure, and this document says so plainly. 15 is an
engineering choice **inside** that principle:

- **Reachable.** The owner's phone reports 12.8, so the gate actually fires. 3 and 5 never do, and a gate that
  never fires is a refusal screen wearing a disguise.
- **Tighter than the floor.** Well inside the 45-degree validity boundary, so it is "seeking the middle" rather
  than scraping the limit.
- **Inside the platform's own judgement** of a good reading (0 to 20), and matching what production apps use.
- **Honest about the instrument.** It is the best this hardware can promise, which is exactly what
  `الميسور لا يسقط بالمعسور` asks of it.

## 10. What this research got WRONG, recorded so it is not repeated

1. **This session recommended 15 degrees once on bad reasoning**, citing 17 and 30-degree mosques as licence. The
   red team destroyed that: those rulings are conditioned on existing buildings, and a device makes a new
   determination. 15 survives, but on the attainability principle, not on mosque leniency.
2. **This session reported only the Egyptian ±45 pole** as though it were the whole picture, until the red team
   surfaced Malaysia's 3. That was a real failure of balance.
3. **A researcher claimed Islamweb fatwa 380437 "does not exist".** It does: this session fetched it. It is dated
   5-8-2018 and is specifically about a phone qibla app deviating "several degrees".
4. **The Ibn Abidin quote carried into round 2 was misworded.** `سيفيد غلبة الظن` is not in *Radd al-Muhtar*. The
   real passage reads `فإنها إن لم تفد اليقين تفد غلبة الظن... وغلبة الظن كافية في ذلك`, and it is about the
   quadrant and astrolabe.
5. **The "between east and west" hadith restriction is Ibn RAJAB's, not Ibn Hajar al-Asqalani's.** A citation
   audit caught the confusion; the two Ibn Hajars are routinely conflated.
6. ***Ahsan al-Fatawa* 2/313, cited by four sources as the origin of the Hanafi 45**, could not be read by anyone:
   the scan is lending-restricted. It remains the weakest link in the 45-degree chain.

## 11. One consequence the owner should decide deliberately

Under the relied-upon Shafi'i view, **certainty of a specific past error creates an obligation to repeat that
prayer** (al-Majmu' 3/207; Mughni al-Muhtaj 1/338-339 via the Palestinian Islamic Council, no. 1403).

So a readout that tells a user a past bearing was wrong is itself fiqh-loaded, not merely informational. The
prototype's diagnostic readout is temporary and comes off before release; a permanent one would be a decision
about more than UI.
