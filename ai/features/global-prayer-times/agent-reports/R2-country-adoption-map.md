# R2: what people actually pray by, country by country

**Status: RESEARCH ONLY.** This report is an adoption study. It answers "which convention do the mosques and the
state in this country actually use", not "what are the published parameters of each authority". The authority
parameter table is R1's subject and is not repeated here.

Research window 2026-09-30. All fetch dates in this report are 2026-09-30 unless a row states otherwise. Web
access was through the `tinyfish` MCP (`tools.tinyfish.search` and `tools.tinyfish.fetch_content`) except where a
page was JavaScript-rendered, where the `agent-browser` CLI was used and the row says so. No page in this report
was read through `curl`.

## Findings in one page

1. **The most important structural finding is the opposite of what was expected.** Several national authorities
   that publish a fixed annual table are nonetheless reproducible by calculation to within a minute, because the
   table was itself generated by a computable rule. Morocco's `habous.gov.ma` table and Saudi Arabia's
   `ummulqura.org.sa` table were both measured against computed times and agreed to 0 or 1 minute across sampled
   dates. The published table and the computable method are not opposed categories. What matters per country is
   whether the rule behind the table is documented and whether local offsets are published.
2. **The single largest and most under-appreciated failure mode is a row-label mismatch, not an angle
   disagreement.** In Malaysia and Indonesia the official first row is `Imsak`, and the official second row is
   `Subuh`, exactly 10 minutes later. A calculation library's `Fajr` output lands on the official `Imsak`, not on
   the official `Subuh`. Measured for JAKIM zone `WLY01` and `SGR01`, computed Fajr sits 8 to 10 minutes before
   official Subuh every day of the sampled month, while sitting within 0 to 2 minutes of official Imsak. An app
   that shows a computed Fajr to a Malaysian user is showing them their Imsak and calling it their prayer time.
3. **Turkey is the mirror image of the same trap.** Diyanet's own table has no Fajr row at all. Its first row is
   literally `İmsak`, and that row is the Fajr prayer time, not a pre-dawn caution margin. Diyanet abolished the
   `temkin` safety margin in 1982 and moved from 19 to 18 degrees. Competing Turkish calendars such as
   `Fazilet Takvimi` still print the old 20-minute `temkin`, and say so in writing, so two Turkish timetables for
   the same city differ by about 20 minutes at Imsak by design.
4. **Madhhab folklore does not survive contact with the printed tables.** Turkey is Hanafi in fiqh and prints the
   standard one-shadow Asr. Measured for Istanbul on 2026-09-30, Diyanet published `İkindi` 16:18 while the
   Hanafi two-shadow computation gives 17:07, a 49-minute gap that rules the Hanafi reading out. Saudi Arabia
   likewise prints standard Asr, measured to 0 minutes against the one-shadow computation and 55 minutes away
   from the two-shadow one. The genuinely Hanafi-Asr territory is the Indian subcontinent diaspora and its
   institutions, not the Hanafi-majority states.
5. **Some timetables print both Asr columns, which means a single Asr setting cannot satisfy them.** East London
   Mosque's published timetable carries `1 Mithl` and `2 Mithl` columns side by side, and `Wifaqul Ulama` in
   Britain publishes explicit definitions for both `Aṣr (Shaf'ae)` at factor 1 and `Aṣr (Hanafi)` at factor 2.
   This is a UI and data-model requirement, not a preference setting.
6. **The mosque supply chain has a documented hand-edit path, and that is the decisive fact for accuracy
   claims.** Every mosque-facing platform read for this report offers both a calculation mode and a bulk upload
   or per-prayer override of the times. Masjidbox tells mosque admins they may "overwrite the time of each
   prayer" and set "minimum, fixed, and maximum values". Masjidal tells them to "mass upload your timings using
   the provided template". Mawaqit states plainly that mosques may "enter their own calendar of prayer times
   directly", and that they sometimes do so precisely to match their country's ministry. No calculation library
   can reproduce a hand-edited or uploaded table by any means.
7. **A congregation time distinct from the start time is a first-class column, not an add-on.** East London
   Mosque prints `Begins` and `Jamā'ah` for every prayer. `londonprayertimes.com`, this app's current provider,
   returns a `Jama'ah` column. Masjidbox exposes "Athan times (Start)" and "Iqamah Time (Jamaah)" as separate
   configurable things. A worldwide app that models only one time per prayer cannot express what a mosque
   timetable actually says.
8. **The app's own London timetable is a mosque-adopted unified table, not a national method, and its own
   publisher says so.** The `Unified Prayer Timetable for London` was adopted from 1 Ramadan 1432 (1 August
   2011) by a group of London mosques, is based on Khalid Shaukat's observation-guided model, is explicitly
   described by its publishers as "a voluntary initiative", is scoped to "the region enclosed by the M25", and
   tells a reader whose local mosque differs to "follow your local mosque". There is no UK national method to
   generalise from.
9. **The ihtiyat safety margin is real, published, and small enough to be mistaken for noise.** Indonesia's
   Kemenag criteria add 2 minutes of `ihtiyati` to every prayer and set Imsak 10 minutes before Subuh. Measured
   against computed Kemenag-parameter times for Jakarta across September 2026, every prayer sat 2 to 4 minutes
   away, in the direction the documented margin predicts. This is a systematic bias, not random error.
10. **Where a country genuinely has no single answer, saying so is the finding.** Nigeria's NSCIA describes
    itself as the apex Islamic authority and is the body that announces the Hijri month, but no national prayer
    timetable or method was located on its own site. India has no national authority of the same kind. Those are
    recorded as honest gaps, not filled in from a neighbour.

## Evidence tiers used throughout

Every country row carries a tier. The tier is a claim about the evidence, not about the country.

| Tier | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| A | The country's own official religious authority publishes prayer times or a method, and it was read on that authority's own site. The site, URL, fetch date and the actual times or parameters seen are named. |
| B | A major national mosque, or the country's largest mosque network, publishes a timetable that was read, and it matches a known convention. |
| C | A widely used local app or website states the convention, or a published academic or technical paper on that country's timetable states it. |
| D | Only a software library's country-to-method mapping asserts it. This is the weakest tier. These mappings are frequently wrong and are copied between projects, so a tier D row is a lead, not a finding. |
| NULL | Nothing could be sourced. Stated as NULL. Not guessed, and not inferred from a neighbouring country. |

A tier D or NULL row is never presented in this report as if it were established. Section "UNVERIFIED and NULL"
lists every such country in one place.

## 1. Where the state's timetable and the mosques' practice diverge

The divergence is not an edge case. In several of the largest Muslim populations the state or the leading council
publishes one thing and mosques print another, and in the countries where this app has most of its current users
there is no state timetable at all.

**The United States.** The Fiqh Council of North America held sessions in 2016 and 2017 and concluded that
"since none of the calculation methods are entirely accurate for all locations across North America, an
approximation must be made". Its published answer is 15 degrees for both Fajr and Isha in the USA, and it states
that this "corresponds to Islamic Society of North America in most prayer apps". Two things about that ruling
matter for adoption. First, it is phrased as a suggestion, not a binding standard: FCNA "suggests" the angle and
"reminds Muslims who are able to determine the timings more precisely in their city and season through actual
observations to consider exercising caution". Second, FCNA itself acknowledges the competing position in the same
document, noting that "decades of research conducted by organizations such as moonsighting.com" found the timings
"are not scientifically deterministic and vary with season, latitude, elevation, and other factors". The
practical result is that ISNA 15 degrees, plain 18 degrees, and the `moonsighting.com` model all have live
constituencies in American masjids, and a masjid's printed Fajr can differ from a computed ISNA Fajr by the full
15-to-18-degree spread. The ISNA label in a settings menu is a calculation choice, not a statement that the
user's local masjid follows it.

**Canada.** The same FCNA document sets Canada apart at 13 degrees for both Fajr and Isha. That is a distinct
national recommendation, and it is a reminder that a single North America setting is wrong. Whether Canadian
mosques actually print 13 degrees was not established. What was established is that the largest Canadian network
prints something a calculation cannot produce: ISNA-Jami Mosque in Toronto lists Fajr at 06:30 with an `Athan`
time of 05:42, and Isha at 08:45 with an Athan of 08:30. The 06:30 figure is an administrative congregation time,
fixed for convenience, and no angle produces it.

**The United Kingdom, including this app's own timetable.** There is no UK national method. The London table this
app currently ships is a mosque-adopted unified table, and its publishers describe it in exactly those terms. The
announcement states that "a number of Masajid and Islamic Centres in London have met several times over the last
eighteen months", that the result "was adopted from 1 Ramadan 1432 (1 August 2011)", and that "it is a voluntary
initiative; each Mosque or Centre is free to make its own choice". It is built on "a mushada-guided computation
model developed and maintained by specialist Khalid Shaukat of moonsighting.com", which "applies actual mushada
(observation) data to determining Fajr and Isha'a times". Its own scope statement is geographic and narrow: "If
you are within the region enclosed by the M25, which is all of London and more, then you can use these times."
And the announcement answers the divergence question directly. Asked "I live in London but my local Mosque has
different times, what should I do", the answer is "You should follow your local mosque". The disagreement is also
public and substantive: `islam21c.com` argues that this timetable "has adopted times which are in opposition to
virtually all the Major fiqh councils from across the Muslim world", which is a live UK dispute rather than a
settled national position.

**India.** No Indian national authority publishing a prayer-time method was located. The widely repeated claim
that India uses the University of Islamic Sciences, Karachi convention was found only in third-party apps and in
a North American council's regional attribution table, never on an Indian body's own site. Indian practice is set
by local mosque committees, and mosque-level changes are announced directly to congregations rather than through a
national table. This country is therefore recorded as UNVERIFIED at country level, which is itself the finding:
there is no single Indian answer for the app to adopt.

**Pakistan.** The Karachi 18 / 18 attribution is genuinely a university convention rather than a state timetable.
The naming is a clue: the method is named for the University of Islamic Sciences, Karachi, not for a ministry. No
Pakistani state authority page publishing the parameters was read in this wave, so the country-level convention is
recorded at tier C. The separate and better-evidenced Pakistani fact is the Asr column: subcontinental practice
prints Hanafi Asr, and the gap is large. Measured for Islamabad on 2026-09-30 with the Karachi parameters, the
standard Asr is 15:22 and the Hanafi Asr is 16:12, a difference of 50 minutes. A wrong Asr setting in Pakistan is
not a rounding error, it is most of an hour.

**Turkey.** The divergence here is between the state and rival calendars rather than between state and mosque.
Diyanet changed its own basis in 1982 and 1983: it lowered Imsak from 19 to 18 degrees and abolished the `temkin`
margin entirely. `Fazilet Takvimi` publishes the before-and-after in its own FAQ, showing Ankara Imsak moving from
06:18 on 31 December 1982 to 06:38 on 1 January 1983, a 20-minute jump, and states that it continues the
tradition: "imsak ile sabah arasındaki 20 dakika temkini devam ettirmektedir" (it continues the 20-minute temkin
between imsak and dawn). Reading a machine-assisted translation of the Turkish, the Süleymaniye Vakfı account
agrees on the mechanism and notes that some Turkish calendars therefore print Imsak about 20 minutes earlier than
Diyanet's. So two Turkish timetables for the same city, both claiming scholarly grounding, differ by about 20
minutes at Imsak by design.

## 2. Countries that publish a TABLE rather than a METHOD

This section corrects an assumption worth correcting, because it changes what the app has to build.

The expectation was that a country publishing fixed tables cannot be reproduced by a calculation library at any
accuracy. That turned out to be false for the two cases measured most carefully. The distinction that actually
matters is not table versus method. It is whether the rule behind the table is recoverable, and whether the
authority publishes local offsets that a coordinate-based library will not apply.

**Morocco, measured.** The Ministry of Habous publishes a per-city monthly Arabic table with no stated angles,
which is the classic profile of an unreproducible table. It reproduces almost exactly. Comparing the ministry's
own Rabat table against computed times using the Morocco parameters (Fajr 19 deg, Isha 17 deg, with Dhuhr and
Maghrib offsets of 5 minutes), for five sampled dates:

| Date (2026) | Prayer deltas, computed minus published, in minutes |
| --- | --- |
| 20 Sep | Fajr 0, Sunrise +3, Dhuhr 0, Asr +1, Maghrib +1, Isha 0 |
| 29 Sep | Fajr 0, Sunrise +3, Dhuhr 0, Asr +1, Maghrib +1, Isha 0 |
| 5 Oct | Fajr +1, Sunrise +3, Dhuhr 0, Asr +1, Maghrib +1, Isha 0 |
| 12 Oct | Fajr 0, Sunrise +3, Dhuhr 0, Asr +1, Maghrib +1, Isha 0 |

Sources: published table `https://www.habous.gov.ma/prieres/horaire_hijri_2.php?ville=1` (Rabat), fetched
2026-09-30, against computed times from `api.aladhan.com` method 21 for 34.0209 N, 6.8416 W. Measured by this
agent. The residual is 0 to 1 minute on the five prayers and a consistent 3 minutes on sunrise. Morocco is
therefore reproducible, and the honest description is "a published table generated by a recoverable rule".

**Saudi Arabia, measured.** The Umm al-Qura calendar is an official published table. For Mecca on 2026-09-30 the
site showed Fajr 04:56, Sunrise 06:11, Dhuhr 12:11, Asr 15:35, Maghrib 18:10, Isha 19:40. Computed Umm al-Qura
for the same day and coordinates gave Fajr 04:56, Sunrise 06:12, Dhuhr 12:11, Asr 15:35, Maghrib 18:10, Isha
19:40. Every prayer matched to 0 minutes, with sunrise 1 minute out. Sources: `ummulqura.org.sa` read with
`agent-browser`, 2026-09-30, against `api.aladhan.com` method 4 for 21.4225 N, 39.8262 E. Measured by this agent.

**Qatar, measured.** The Ministry of Interior endpoint served Fajer 4:00, Sunrise 5:18, Dhuhr 11:32, Asr 3:02,
Maghrib 5:47, Isha 7:17 for Doha. Computed Qatar-convention times for the same date matched within 1 minute on
every prayer and 2 minutes on sunrise. Source: `portal.moi.gov.qa` endpoint, 2026-09-30, against
`api.aladhan.com` method 10. Measured by this agent.

**Where a published table genuinely defeats calculation.** Three mechanisms were evidenced, and none of them is
about the twilight angle.

1. **A published local offset that no coordinate library applies.** Brunei's Ministry of Religious Affairs states
   on its own timetable page that Belait district must add 3 minutes and Tutong district must add 1 minute. A
   library given Belait's true coordinates will not produce the official Belait time, because the official time is
   the national table plus a decreed constant.
2. **A zone system instead of coordinates.** JAKIM publishes by zone, with more than 60 named zones such as
   `SGR01` and `WLY01`, each covering several districts. The official time for a village is its zone's time, not
   the time computed at the village. A coordinate-based library is answering a different question from the one the
   national timetable answers.
3. **A documented safety margin applied to every row.** Indonesia's Kemenag criteria add 2 minutes of `ihtiyati`
   to Subuh, Dhuha, Zuhur, Ashar, Maghrib and Isha, and subtract 2 minutes from sunrise. Measured against
   Kemenag-parameter computation for Jakarta across all 30 days of September 2026, the published times sat 2 to 4
   minutes from computed on every prayer, in the direction the margin predicts: Subuh 2 to 3 minutes late, Dzuhur
   3 to 4 minutes late, Ashar 3 to 4, Maghrib 3 to 4, Isya 2 to 3, and sunrise 3 to 4 minutes early. Sources:
   `https://api.myquran.com/v2/sholat/jadwal/1301/2026/9` serving the Kemenag-derived Jakarta schedule, against
   `api.aladhan.com` method 20. Measured by this agent. The margin is small but it is systematic, so it never
   averages out.

**The Malaysia row-label result, measured, and the most consequential number in this report.** For JAKIM zone
`SGR01` across all 30 days of September 2026, comparing JAKIM's official API against computed JAKIM-parameter
times:

| Quantity | Measured result across 30 days |
| --- | --- |
| Official `imsak` to official `fajr` gap | exactly 10 minutes, every day, no exceptions |
| Computed Fajr minus official `fajr` (Subuh) | between -8 and -10 minutes |
| Computed Fajr minus official `imsak` | between 0 and +2 minutes |
| Computed Asr minus official `asr` | -1 to -2 minutes |

Sources: `https://www.e-solat.gov.my/index.php?r=esolatApi/takwimsolat&period=month&zone=SGR01`, 2026-09-30,

important part. A library configured to JAKIM's own published angles does not land on JAKIM's published Subuh at

words: "Kementerian Agama menggunakan waktu imsak sepuluh menit sebelum masuk waktu subuh" (the Ministry uses an
imsak time ten minutes before the entry of subuh).

## 3. The madhhab map

The folklore is that the Hanafi world prays Hanafi Asr. The printed tables say otherwise, and the evidence is
measurement rather than assertion, because the two Asr conventions are roughly an hour apart and so impossible to
confuse once measured.

**Hanafi-majority states that print the standard one-shadow Asr.**

| Country | Published Asr | Standard computed Asr | Hanafi computed Asr | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Turkey (Istanbul, 2026-09-30) | 16:18 | 16:18 | 17:07 | Standard Asr, 49-minute margin rules out Hanafi |
| Saudi Arabia (Mecca, 2026-09-30) | 15:35 | 15:35 | 16:30 | Standard Asr, 55-minute margin |
| Qatar (Doha, 2026-09-07) | 15:02 | 15:01 | 16:01 | Standard Asr, 59-minute margin |

Sources as in the country table. Measured by this agent. Turkey is the instructive case: the Turkish religious
establishment is Hanafi, and Diyanet's timetable nonetheless prints the one-shadow Asr. "Hanafi country" and
"Hanafi Asr timetable" are independent facts.

**Where Hanafi Asr genuinely is the printed norm.** The evidenced home of the two-shadow Asr is the Indian
subcontinent and its diaspora institutions, not the Hanafi-majority states. `Wifaqul Ulama` in Britain, a
Deobandi-tradition body, publishes both and defines them precisely: "Aṣr (Shaf'ae): This is calculated when the
shadow reaches a factor of 1 plus the original length of the shadow at Istiwāʾ" and "Aṣr (Hanafi): This is
calculated when the shadow reaches a factor of 2". For Pakistan the measured gap at Islamabad on 2026-09-30 is
15:22 standard against 16:12 Hanafi, 50 minutes.

**Countries whose official or dominant timetable prints BOTH Asr columns.** This is the case that defeats a
single Asr setting, and it is evidenced in the app's own home market. East London Mosque's published timetable
carries `1 Mithl` and `2 Mithl` as two separate Asr columns alongside `Begins` and `Jamā'ah`. `Wifaqul Ulama`
publishes both factors for the same city. Mosque platforms have institutionalised the choice: Masjidbox instructs
admins that "In the Asr Prayer, and according to your school, you can choose between Shafi and Hanafi", and
Masjidal exposes an "asr method" setting. So in the UK the answer to "which Asr" is "both, printed side by side",
and a data model with one Asr field cannot represent the source timetable.

**A third Asr convention exists and is not a rounding of the other two.** `moonsighting.com` records the Asr
shadow factor as "4/7 for Shi'aa; 1 for Shafi'i, Maaliki, Hanbali, and 2 for Hanafi". Shia-majority countries are
therefore a distinct third case, not a variant of the standard one. The same source records a different Maghrib
too: "For Sunni's, actual sunset is 3 minutes after theoretical sunset; for Shi'aas it is 17 minutes after
theoretical sunset." Iran, Iraq and Azerbaijan cannot be served correctly by a Sunni Asr and a sunset Maghrib.

## 4. Imsak, and other extra rows

The prior moonsighting wave established that Indonesian and Malay timetables print Subuh with Imsak about 10
minutes earlier, and that Turkish timetables print Imsak where the English prints Fajr. Both claims are confirmed,
one of them made exact, and the picture extends further.

**Confirmed and made exact: Malaysia, 10 minutes, invariant.** JAKIM's official API returns `imsak` and `fajr` as
separate fields. Across 30 consecutive days for zone `SGR01` the gap was exactly 10 minutes on every single day,
with no seasonal variation. This is a fixed decreed offset, not a computed quantity.

**Confirmed and sourced to the authority's own words: Indonesia, 10 minutes.** Kemenag's criteria list opens with
"Imsak 10 menit sebelum waktu Subuh". Kemenag published its reasoning separately: the Hisab Rukyat team agreed to
read the Prophetic report of the gap between finishing the pre-dawn meal and beginning prayer, described as "the
length of time a person reads 50 verses", as a 10-minute duration, and Kemenag describes the result as "katup
pengaman (tindakan hati-hati)", a safety valve. Measured against Kemenag's own Jakarta schedule, the gap was 10
minutes on all 30 sampled days.

**Confirmed, and stronger than stated: Turkey does not print Fajr at all.** Diyanet's table has six rows and the
first is `İmsak`. There is no separate Fajr row to compare it against. So in the Turkish tradition Imsak is not an
extra row before Fajr, it is the name of the Fajr row. This is the opposite structural situation from Malaysia and
Indonesia, where Imsak is a genuine extra row 10 minutes earlier, and conflating them produces a 10-minute error
in one direction or the other.

**Extension: the Turkish Imsak is itself contested by 20 minutes.** Because Diyanet abolished `temkin` in 1982
while `Fazilet Takvimi` kept it, the Turkish `İmsak` row exists in two published versions about 20 minutes apart.
Fazilet documents the exact transition in its own FAQ table.

**Extension: Imsak is a purchasable hardware feature in Indonesia.** Indonesian mosque-clock vendors sell the
Imsak row as a priced add-on. `jadwalsholatdigital.com` lists "Tambah Imsak" against each model with an explicit
surcharge, for example a base `Jadwal Shalat Classic A` at Rp 1,350,000 becoming Rp 1,700,000 with Imsak added,
and states that "Untuk Penambahan Jadwal Waktu Imsak ... dikenakan tambahan biaya khusus sesuai ukuran" (adding
the Imsak time incurs an additional charge according to size). A row that mosques pay extra to display is not
cosmetic.

**Other countries printing Imsak as a distinct row.** Brunei's official `Jadual Waktu Sembahyang` carries `Imsak`
and `Suboh` as separate columns. MUIS Singapore publishes a Ramadan `Imsakiah`. Bahrain's government service
description for the official `Islamiyat` app names "Prayers timing and notifications and the Imsakya timings
during Ramadan", so Imsak there is a Ramadan-scoped row rather than a year-round one. DITIB publishes a
`Ramazan İmsakiyesi` for Germany.

**Other extra rows that a five-prayer model cannot hold.** Malaysia prints `Syuruk` and `Duha` as named rows,
Brunei prints `Syuruk` and `Doha`, Indonesia's Kemenag criteria define `Dhuha` at the sun 4.5 degrees above the
horizon, and Turkey publishes `Kıble Saati` (a qibla time) plus astronomical sunrise and sunset distinct from the
`Güneş` and `Akşam` prayer rows. None of these are prayers, and all of them appear on the official timetable the
user expects to see.

## 5. How mosque clocks actually get their times

This was an explicit owner question, and the answer is the most decisive accuracy finding in the report. There are
four distinct supply paths, and three of the four break the assumption that a computed time can match a mosque's
printed time.

**Path 1: a hardware clock with a built-in algorithm and a city table.** The dominant consumer and small-mosque
product is the Al-Harameen azan wall clock family. Its own product pages describe the data path plainly: "Prayer
times for most cities around the world", "Easy setup: program your city into the clock", "Cities are selected by
international telephone dialing code". The fallback for an unlisted location is manual coordinate entry: "If your
city is not among them, enter its coordinates (longitude and latitude) and the GMT offset, and the clock works out
its prayer times and displays them automatically." So these devices compute, from a preloaded city table keyed by
dialling code, with no network sync and no connection to the national authority's table. A mosque using one is not
displaying its ministry's times unless the two happen to agree.

**Path 3: copying the national authority's annual table.** This path is documented from the platform side, which
is the strongest form of the evidence because it describes what mosques actually asked for. Mawaqit's help centre
states that mosques choosing to upload their own calendar "sometimes want the same calendar as their city (in
Arabic countries for example) or as The Ministry of Religious Affairs of their country (like in Turkey)". The
national artefacts that make this possible were located: Diyanet's own per-city yearly tables at
`namazvakitleri.diyanet.gov.tr` including foreign cities such as Berlin, JAKIM's zone API and `takwim`, Kemenag's
`Jadwal Shalat` and `Jadwal Imsakiyah` at `bimasislam.kemenag.go.id`, Morocco's per-city monthly Habous tables,
Brunei's monthly ministry table, and MUIS Singapore's full-year `PRAYER TIMES FOR SINGAPORE` PDF.

**Path 4: a fixed administrative congregation time that is not derived from the sun at all.** ISNA-Jami Mosque in
Toronto prints Fajr 06:30 against an Athan of 05:42. The 06:30 is chosen for the congregation's convenience. The
Cairo mosque on Mawaqit publishes fixed jamaah offsets of +25, +20, +20, +10 and +20 minutes. London
timetables print `Begins` and `Jamā'ah` as separate columns. No calculation reproduces these, and for a user
attending that mosque the jamaah time is the one that governs their day.

**What the supply chain means for the app.** A computed time can match a national authority's published table,
sometimes exactly, as Morocco, Saudi Arabia and Qatar demonstrate. A computed time cannot in principle match an
individual mosque's printed table, because the mosque may have uploaded a spreadsheet, applied per-prayer
overrides, or set a fixed congregation time. Any accuracy claim the app makes should therefore be scoped to the
authority, never to the mosque.

## Method notes and limitations

- Every delta in this report was computed by this agent by comparing an authority's own published times against
  computed times from `api.aladhan.com`, and is labelled measured. `api.aladhan.com` was used as a calculation
  engine to test reproducibility, not as an authority. No prayer time in this report was copied, averaged or
  invented.
- One artefact is worth recording so it is not mistaken for a finding. For Morocco, the computed calendar for
  13 September 2026 sat exactly 60 minutes from the published table while adjacent dates sat within 1 minute.
  Re-querying the single date directly reproduced the same one-hour shift, which identifies it as a daylight-saving
  boundary artefact in the calculation service for `Africa/Casablanca`, not a difference in the ministry's method.
  Morocco's reproducibility result is stated from the dates outside that boundary.
- Arabic, Turkish, Malay and Indonesian sources were read in the original language. The Turkish `temkin` history
  from `suleymaniyevakfi.org` was read with machine translation assistance and is flagged as such in the text
  where it is used. The Malay and Indonesian material required no translation aid for the terms that matter
  (`imsak`, `subuh`, `ihtiyati`, `asar`, `isyak`).
- `awqaf.gov.ae` (UAE), `ummulqura.org.sa` and `awqaf.gov.jo` are JavaScript-rendered. The Saudi table was
  recovered with `agent-browser`. The UAE table was not recovered in this wave despite both `tinyfish` and
  `agent-browser` attempts, so the UAE's official times are not reported at tier A here.
- Scope honesty: this agent verified the countries in the first country table directly. All other countries come
  from the three regional cluster sub-agents, carry their own tiers, and are reproduced with their sources intact.
