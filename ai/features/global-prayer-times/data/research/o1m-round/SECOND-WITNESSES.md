# O1m-45 — second witnesses for the 30 single-family countries

Wave O1m-45, round o1m, worldwide prayer-times census. Workspace
`$HOME/athan-gitree/census/o1m/o1m-45-singles/`. Entry state: `o1m-26-consensus` classified
thirty countries as `SINGLE`, each served by `athanpro` alone. This wave finds a second publisher
for twenty-five of them, measures the pair on the same settlement and the same dates, and names
what each pair proves.

Engine for every number: **adhan 4.4.6** at
`$HOME/athan-gitree/census/adhan-build/node_modules/adhan`, its `SolarTime` core read directly so
the package's high-latitude substitution cannot fabricate a twilight, `Rounding.None`. Zones are
pinned per city, dates come from the rows themselves, and no clock is read anywhere.

## 3. The measurement

**same thirty dates** (the athanpro cycle is one frozen September 2026), measured at the
settlement's published coordinate. Both publishers carry the same place name, so the shared point
is honest; turktakvim publishes no coordinates, and its own solar columns carry its printed
margins, so a point solved from its rows would absorb the margin into latitude. Rows whose solar
columns leave the place by more than twenty minutes are screened out and counted (§6).

Offsets are **turktakvim minus athanpro**, in minutes, median over the paired days.

## 4. What the pairs prove

### 4.1 The Turkish ladder is a publisher attribute, and it holds on twenty territories

Sixteen of the twenty-three turktakvim pairs return the **same six numbers**: fajr 0 to +1,
sunrise −5, dhuhr +7, asr +7, maghrib +6, isha +7. That is the ladder the consensus measured
across turktakvim's nine comparable countries (sunrise −4.5, dhuhr +7, asr +7, maghrib +5.5,
isha +7.3), reproduced here on the Caribbean, the Gulf of Guinea, the Congo basin and the central
Pacific, from a different sample, a different month and a different set of towns.

The counterpart is athanpro's zero ladder: its own sunrise, dhuhr and maghrib sit **within one
minute of the engine at its own published point in twenty-two of the twenty-three countries**
(Western Sahara is the exception, §4.3). That is what makes the pair a clean two-ladder
comparison rather than a comparison of two unknown recipes.

Fajr is the column where the two publishers agree, and they agree everywhere the athanpro page is
not a mosque's rule: **100 per cent of paired days within one minute in twenty of the
twenty-three countries**, and in those twenty the worst single day is 1 minute. Two publishers
with a different margin ladder still put fajr on the same 18° twilight at 0.2 N, 19 N and 54 S.

### 4.2 The isha column is an angle, not a margin

The isha offset is stable inside every country — range 1 minute over the month — which alone would
read as a margin. Across latitude it is not stable:

| |latitude| | country | isha offset |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0.2 N | sao-tome-and-principe | +7.0 |
| 0.5 N | nauru | +7.0 |
| 4.3 S | republic-of-the-congo | +7.0 |
| 8.5 S | tuvalu | +7.0 |
| 12.1 N | caribbean-netherlands | +7.0 |
| 19.3 N | cayman-islands | +7.0 |
| 29.0 S | norfolk-island | +8.0 |
| 38.6 N | tajikistan | +9.0 |
| 51.7 S | falkland-islands | +13.0 |
| 54.3 S | south-georgia | +15.0 |

A printed temkin is flat with latitude; a deeper twilight angle is not. Seven minutes at the
equator becomes fifteen at 54 degrees south, which is the signature of roughly one degree of
twilight: turktakvim's effective isha sits near 18.5 to 19 degrees where athanpro's sits at 17.05.
The consensus saw the same split as a margin in its nine turktakvim countries because those sit
between 20 and 40 degrees, where a degree looks like seven minutes.

Consequence for the class: below about 45 degrees the isha difference is indistinguishable from a
margin over one month, and the wave classes those countries MARGIN DISPUTE. Above it, the isha
column passes twelve minutes, past any printed temkin, and the wave classes the country
CONVENTION DISPUTE and names isha. The column-level reading is one statement: **isha is a
convention difference in all twenty-three pairs; the class records only where it is provable from
one month at one latitude.**

### 4.3 The four genuine convention disputes

- **san-marino** — athanpro's San Marino pages carry a **14.9 / 14.95 pair**, not the platform's
  18/17: fajr 18 minutes earlier, isha 22 minutes later than turktakvim. This is the class the
  consensus found on Monaco (UMAM 14/14) and Liechtenstein (14/14), and it reaches San Marino.
  turktakvim holds the ordinary 18/18.7.
- **sint-maarten** — athanpro's Philipsburg page is a **15/15** rule against turktakvim's 18/18.7:
  fajr 12 minutes earlier, isha 16 minutes later. A mosque-tier rule, not the platform's.
- **falkland-islands** and **south-georgia** — the offsets are not the ladder but the ladder plus a
  point error: turktakvim's Stanley table prints a day **19 minutes longer** than Stanley's own sun
  and a noon 6 minutes off its meridian; Grytviken likewise (20 minutes and 7 minutes). The
  twilight columns still land within 1 minute of athanpro's, so the tables are the same convention
  computed at a different place. Recorded as a table-quality note on turktakvim, not as a dispute
  about the sky.
- **western-sahara** — not a convention dispute at all but a **clock dispute**: athanpro computes
  the territory at UTC for the whole month while Africa/El_Aaiun runs UTC+1, and turktakvim prints
  UTC+1 for 1-19 September and UTC for 20-30 September. The eleven straight-clock days give fajr
  +5, sunrise −2, dhuhr +3, asr +8, maghrib +2, isha +8. Neither publisher's Western Sahara page
  can be read as a clock without this note.

### 4.4 The three countries with three or two surfaces and a story

- **tajikistan** — three surfaces now: athanpro, turktakvim (316 cities, 115,340 dated rows,
  Khujand, Khorog, Kulob, Qurghonteppa, Vahdat, Dushanbe among them) and the authority
  `shuroiulamo.tj`, already banked with 4,328 rows across 2014-2026. The pair is the widest ladder
  in the set (dhuhr +9, isha +9): turktakvim's Tajik layer prints dhuhr at +9 rather than its usual
  +7, and its own Dushanbe row is computed about four minutes west of the rest of the country
  (sunrise −10.7 there against −6.3 at Khujand), so the country median rests on seven cities rather
  than one. athanpro's ten Tajik pages are on its zero ladder to within 0.2 minutes.
- **saint-helena-ascension-and-tristan-da-cunha** — three families. turktakvim and athanpro both
  serve Jamestown; on Ascension, turktakvim and Diyanet (Georgetown) agree to within a stable
  ladder: fajr 0, sunrise +2, dhuhr +1.5, asr +2, maghrib −2, isha +7. That is the smallest
  spread of any pair in the wave and it puts a second and third family on a territory that had one.
- **svalbard-and-jan-mayen** — Diyanet Longyearbyen against athanpro Longyearbyen: sunrise +8,
  dhuhr −5, maghrib −13 but fajr **−49.5** and isha **+48.5** with a range of 40 to 50 minutes
  inside the month. At 78 N in September the two publishers apply different polar substitutions,
  and the size of the disagreement is the finding: the territory now has two dated surfaces and
  they cannot be reconciled by any margin.
- **jersey** — the Islamic Centre's own 2026 sheet against athanpro Saint Helier: fajr 0, sunrise
  0, dhuhr −4, asr +1.5, maghrib −5, **isha +14**. The mosque runs a 17.9 / 15.1 pair with a
  dhuhr +4.5 and maghrib +5.5 clock convention (the o1m-12 fit); athanpro runs 18/17 at zero
  margins but with the **same asr school**, so its Jersey pages are mosque-sourced rather than
  platform-standard. Community tier, one family, one measured disagreement on isha.

## 6. Defects found, recorded not corrected

1. **turktakvim prints two fajr-adjacent columns.** `imsak` sits 11 to 24 minutes before `sabah`
   (median 15) across the 17,955 rows banked here. `sabah` is the fajr prayer and is what this wave
   measures; a reader who takes the first column as fajr will report a 15-minute convention
   difference that does not exist.
2. **turktakvim computes nothing above the arctic circle.** Its Svalbard entry (7 settlements) and
   its three Tokelau entries return empty tables; Wallis and Futuna (28 settlements) likewise. The
   defect the consensus recorded above ~60 N extends to these island entries.
3. **turktakvim's Stanley and Grytviken tables are computed off the place** (19 and 20 minutes of
   extra day length, 6 and 7 minutes of noon error).
4. **turktakvim's Smara table steps an hour at 2026-09-20** and stays there — UTC+1 for nineteen
   days, UTC for eleven.
5. **athanpro computes Western Sahara at UTC.** Its Es-Semara page is 63 minutes early on sunrise
   for the whole month and its Aousserd page declares `UTC` as its zone.
6. **athanpro's Port Elizabeth (Bequia) page is six hours out.** It prints fajr 10:43 where the
   island's own sun gives 04:44. The row screen caught it and Saint Vincent is measured on
   Kingstown and Barrouallie only.
7. **athanpro's Guinea-Bissau sunrise is coarse.** Two distinct sunrise values across September on
   all three pages while the sunset moves over twenty; the shape is a stepped column, not a
   computed one. The fajr, dhuhr and asr columns carry it, so the pair still measures.
8. **A name collision is waiting in the turktakvim tree.** Its `Port Elizabeth` under Saint
   Vincent is the island's; no other collision was found among the paired names, but the tree
   repeats settlement names across countries and every pair must be matched on the country as well
   as the name.
