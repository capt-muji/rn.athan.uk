## 3. What the pairs prove

1. **The Turkish ladder is a publisher attribute, not a country rule.** Sixteen of the twenty-three
   pairs return the same six numbers — fajr 0 to +1, sunrise −5, dhuhr +7, asr +7, maghrib +6,
   isha +7 — which reproduces the consensus ladder on the Caribbean, the Gulf of Guinea, the Congo
   basin and the Pacific, from a different sample and a different month.
2. **Fajr is one twilight at 18°.** 100 per cent of paired days within 1 minute in twenty of the
   twenty-three countries, worst 1 minute, from 0.2 N to 54 S.
3. **The isha column is an angle, and the wave can date where it stops looking like a margin.** The
   isha offset runs 7 minutes at the equator, 8 at 29 S, 9 at 39 N, 13 at 52 S and 15 at 54 S. A
   temkin is flat with latitude; this is roughly a degree of twilight (18.5-19 against 17.05). Below
   45° a single month cannot separate the two, so the class says MARGIN DISPUTE and the column
   reading says isha is a convention difference everywhere.
4. **athanpro's zero ladder holds in the micro-territories**, within 1 minute on sunrise, dhuhr and
   maghrib in twenty-two of twenty-three countries. Its exceptions are the two pages carrying a
   mosque's rule (San Marino 14.9/14.95, Sint Maarten 15/15), which is the same class as Monaco and
   Liechtenstein in the consensus.
5. **Tajikistan now has three surfaces** — athanpro, turktakvim (316 cities) and the authority
   `shuroiulamo.tj` banked in o1m-10 with 4,328 rows across 2014-2026 — the only one of the thirty
   with an authority tier.

## 5. Defects recorded

turktakvim prints two fajr-adjacent columns (imsak 11-24 minutes before sabah, median 15); its
arctic and Tokelau and Wallis entries compute nothing; its Stanley and Grytviken tables are
computed 19-20 minutes long with a 6-7 minute noon error; its Smara table steps an hour at
2026-09-20. athanpro computes Western Sahara at UTC all month; its Port Elizabeth (Bequia) page is
six hours out and was screened; its Guinea-Bissau sunrise is a stepped column. Details in
`SECOND-WITNESSES.md` §6.
