## The sweep behind the negatives

Council-level searches in Norwegian, Swedish, Danish, German, Dutch, French,
Turkish, Bosnian, Finnish, Russian, Polish, Arabic and Urdu (site-restricted
council/jamiat/rabita/verband/raad/muftiyat terms, 2026-11-11) returned no
national body naming the committee's method for prayer times. The term
"moonsighting" in most hits is crescent-date sighting (Wifaq ul Ulama's
criteria PDF, ISNA's date policy), a different subject. One live event is
recorded from the sweep: **islamweb.no's prayer-times section no longer
publishes the MC tables** - the page now promotes "Nye bønnetider fra IRN"
(IRN's standardized 16/15 times), the site's TLS certificate has expired, and
no method statement exists anywhere on it. Norway's proven adoption therefore
rests on the banked 2026 PDFs, and the publisher's live position has moved to
IRN. The o1k operations calendar should watch islamweb.no for a 2027 edition
either way.

## Verdict

The worldwide map is small and precise. One body publishes the method
(the committee itself). One national council adopted it in writing - FCNA,
2011, in a statement its own current page has since replaced with 15/15 and
13/13. One ulama council publishes it in tables - Norway's Jamiat, whose
2026 tables ride the curve to a third of a minute, and whose site now points
at IRN. Two English town mosques - Aisha Masjid Reading and Golden Mosque Rochdale - publish tables that
are the curve to the minute, the first community-tier proven publishers in
the corpus. One lineage runs through the committee's astronomer into the UK's
national tables (Hizbul Ulama, Blackburn), which is why the whole English
interval family's fajr sits on the curve without any of them following the
committee. Nowhere else - not the Nordics, the Baltics, Russia, continental
Europe, nor any council-level hit in thirteen languages - does a body follow
it. Apps and aggregators carry the name widely; none of them is authority.
