# SPAN-LEDGER — the forward-longevity answer, per recorded country

Agent O1l-A2, 2026-11-11. Workspace `$HOME/athan-gitree/census/o1l-city/span-ledger/`.
Built from the banked record only: no capture was taken, no page was probed, nothing under /tmp,
the repository untouched, no subagent spawned. Every backward-harvest lead below is a named
opportunity handed to the harvest lanes, not a measurement.

The owner's question: **"how do I know the recipes still hold in 20 years?"** This ledger answers it
country by country with two numbers and one risk. The number is the ceiling of what the evidence
proves (a fitted future endpoint, or N past years on one recipe). The risk is the named thing that
would break the recipe and is not covered by that ceiling: an unexercised season, a publisher
generation that could be swapped, a regime change we have dated but not spanned.

## How to read a row

- **Ceiling** — distinct years held · total dated rows · the basis. `dedup` = distinct date-and-column
  slots after de-duplication; `raw` = rows as captured. Where both exist, both are given and named.
- **Recipe** — one line: angles / intervals / margins / rounding / rule-form. Only what a fit
  delivered; `unfitted` means no recipe is claimed.
- **Verdict** — one of the five owner classes, applied strictly:
  - **PROVEN-TO-<year>** — an endpoint serves the future AND the fit held across it.
  - **STABLE-ACROSS-<N>-YEARS** — multi-year past evidence, no future endpoint.
  - **SINGLE-CYCLE** — one published cycle (year, edition, month or short sample).
  - **FROZEN-GENERATION** — byte-identical editions or a perpetual table; the precedent caps it.
  - **RECOMPUTED-SPAN** — the Algeria class: multiple years, one recipe, the years provably recomputed.
  - **FALLBACK** — added class, outside the owner's five: no object, no recipe. Counted separately and
    never folded into SINGLE-CYCLE, because inventing a cycle where no surface exists would be worse
    than saying so.
- **Regime changes** — every dated change on the record, with the split date. A span that crosses one
  is split there and never blended; where the record dates a change only to a season or a year, the
  cell says so.
- **Backward harvest** — the cheapest past year not yet taken, or `none served`.

Source tags: **WT** = `census/world-table-v2.md` row of that name; **FI** = the `FIT-INDEX.json`
entry id; **NS5**, **NS7** = `night-summary/NIGHT-SUMMARY.md` sections 5 and 7; **C** =
`o1k-wide/CONSOLIDATION.md` wave block; **O1l-<n>** = the `o1l-city` sibling report;
**BRIEF** = the O1l binding order. Inline tags mark a cell that disagrees with its neighbours.

Conflict rule: where the fit index and the world table disagree, the row carries both readings and
marks the disagreement `CONFLICT`. The world table predates the fits it summarises in several places,
so the fit index wins on recipe detail and the table wins on tier and provenance; neither is silently
overwritten.

## What this ledger cannot say

- **It measures the record, not the world.** No page was probed for this deliverable. Every "serves"
  claim is the record's, dated 2026-11-10/11; a surface can close between that capture and the next.
- **Rows are not always days.** Several rows carry a count the record never stated (Yemen's 1,064 rows
  cannot convert to days, Komotini's fit restates none, the Lebanese feed is undated, the SADC towns are
  one month each). They are marked unknown, never inferred.
- **The world table lags the fits in eight places**: Algeria (FALLBACK →
  recomputed), Somalia/Somaliland (FALLBACK → community), Latvia/Lithuania/Belarus (FALLBACK → first
  surfaces), Denmark (FALLBACK → three-way), Kosovo's edition count, Kyrgyzstan's span, Niger's grade,
  and Portugal's isha reading. Each row carries its `CONFLICT` marker; none was silently overwritten.
- **A receipt is not a forecast.** Fourteen of the fifteen PROVEN-TO verdicts rest on endpoints that
  serve computed future rows; London's rests on a recovered table that a publisher could replace. They
  prove the publisher's generator is stable, not that the publisher will keep it:
  Kazakhstan's tables are the publisher's own client engine executed locally, so a changed served
  script changes the table with no year boundary. That is the residual risk every PROVEN row carries.
- **The three arbitration items are still open** (Pakistan's anchor — resolved on the record to one
  recipe; Indonesia's two national lines — resolved to two objects, never merged; Niger's grade —

**The forward answer in one line.** Fifteen countries have an evidenced future horizon. The longest
belong to machine endpoints, with one recovered table among them: Kazakhstan 2100, Saudi Arabia and
London 2076, Indonesia and Kyrgyzstan 2035. Eighty-three have one cycle, which is why the same
one-year gap repeats across five continents. Twelve have editions that a reader would mistake for
years and are not.

**The largest raw gap is not the most valuable buy.** South Africa is served to 2031, Maldives to 2036,
Botswana 2021-2026, Niger +6 — and every one of those extra years is a reprint or a recomputation of
one generation. Banking them adds rows, not confidence. The useful gaps are the ten below, ranked by
evidence bought per request.
