# R14 part 4: what happens to the armed alarms when the position changes

The app arms up to **64** OS notification requests (`NOTIFICATION_REQUEST_BUDGET`,
`shared/constants.ts:75`) and its candidate walk may reach **66 days** ahead
(`SCHEDULE_CANDIDATE_DAYS`, `:88`). Both measured in this worktree.

## 4E. The three errors a travelling alarm can carry, separated

An OS notification request holds an absolute instant. A prayer time is a wall-clock
rendering of a solar event at a position. So a move produces three independent errors and
only the first two are the app being wrong:

| error | cause | who fixes it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| solar | the sun rises at a different instant at the new position | a re-arm with the new position |
| zone | the same instant renders as a different wall clock | a re-arm with the new zone |
| both | the common case on any international journey | a re-arm |

## 4F. Per journey: the error in the alarm the OS already holds

`solar min` is how far the true prayer instant at the DESTINATION moves from the instant the
alarm holds. `zone min` is the UTC offset change. `total displayed min` is what a user at the
destination sees on their own clock when the old alarm fires.

| journey               | fajr solar | sunrise solar | dhuhr solar | asr solar | maghrib solar | isha solar | worst solar | zone min | worst total |
| --------------------- | ---------- | ------------- | ----------- | --------- | ------------- | ---------- | ----------- | -------- | ----------- |
| London to Makkah      | -86        | -65           | -160        | -223      | -256          | -239       | 256         | +120     | 376         |
| London to Jeddah      | -84        | -62           | -157        | -221      | -253          | -236       | 253         | +120     | 373         |
| London to Istanbul    | -84        | -72           | -116        | -138      | -162          | -149       | 162         | +120     | 282         |
| London to Manchester  | 1          | -3            | 8           | 12        | 20            | 17         | 20          | +0       | 20          |
| London to Toronto     | 343        | 352           | 317         | 301       | 281           | 291        | 352         | -300     | 652         |
| KualaLumpur to Makkah | 204        | 213           | 247         | 241       | 281           | 290        | 290         | -300     | 590         |
| Dhaka to Makkah       | 210        | 207           | 202         | 204       | 197           | 195        | 210         | -180     | 390         |
| Jeddah to Makkah      | -2         | -3            | -3          | -2        | -3            | -3         | 3           | +0       | 3           |

The London to Makkah row is the one to read: the solar error alone is over three hours,
and the zone error adds another three. An alarm armed in London and left armed in Makkah
does not fire late, it fires at a time that is not any prayer.

## 4G. How many armed requests a source change invalidates, by window

Every request the OS holds for a day after the move is wrong. This is the count, by how far
ahead the budget reached, which depends entirely on how many rows the user has armed.

| user configuration                | requests per day | days the budget reaches | requests invalidated by a move |
| --------------------------------- | ---------------- | ----------------------- | ------------------------------ |
| 1 row, at-time only               | 1                | 64                      | 64                             |
| 3 rows, at-time only              | 3                | 21                      | 63                             |
| 6 rows, at-time only              | 6                | 10                      | 60                             |
| 6 rows, each with one reminder    | 12               | 5                       | 60                             |
| 11 rows, each with one reminder   | 22               | 2                       | 44                             |
| 21 rows, each with both reminders | 63               | 1                       | 63                             |

So the worst case for STALENESS is the lightest user: one row armed at-time only reaches
**64 days ahead**, and every one of those 64 alarms is wrong the moment the user lands. The
heaviest user is paradoxically the safest, because 63 requests buy only 3 days of reach.
