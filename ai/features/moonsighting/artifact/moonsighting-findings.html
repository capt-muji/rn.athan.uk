Moonsighting Method Findings

Can London's unified prayer timetable be rebuilt, to the minute, from Khalid Shaukat's Moonsighting Committee method? And what would a worldwide option rest on? In short: London is not that method plus offsets, and the method's only machine-readable source is one developer's server.

In brief

Seven findings the rest of the page supports. Deltas are in minutes, and no prayer time on this page was computed or invented for it.

  1. London's timetable is not the moonsighting.com method with offsets.Its Fajr and Isha are fixed intervals from sunrise and sunset, taken from the Blackburn observation chart (Miftahi, Fajar and Isha, Tables 5 and 6), with London's own edits. Sunrise is shown 3 minutes early. Dhuhr (+5) and Maghrib (+3) sit on HMNAO sun times.
  2. Against moonsighting.com, London differs on most days.London API minus the method's own table for 2026 (method 0): Fajr −7 to +6, Isha −4 to +11, sunrise −3 or −2, Dhuhr and Maghrib 0 or +1, Asr 0 to +2.
  3. The formula has a primary source.Shaukat's booklet FAJR AND ISHA (September 2015), §11, publishes the coefficients. They are exactly the ones adhan uses. lead-verified
  4. The published tables follow rules no page states in full.A seasonal function bounded by 18°; above 55°, also bounded by 1/7 of the night at the true latitude, with no slide to 60°. In polar night, plain 18° Fajr and Isha; in midnight sun, dashes. A model of this reproduces the tables within ±1 minute below about 66° in both hemispheres.
  5. The method's machine-readable source is fragile.www.moonsighting.com/time_json.php returns HTTP 500. The working copy is on the generator developer's personal host, with no terms. It changes the clock a day early in seven time zones.
  6. moonsighting.com has been dormant since spring 2024.No page publishes the formula, and none mentions London's unified timetable. From 2012 to 2017 it offered, by post, a booklet it said contained the algorithms.
  7. The app has Asr labelled the wrong way round.API asr is the Shafi'i (one shadow) time and asr_2 the Hanafi; shared/types.ts says the reverse. The app shows asr.

Where each timetable comes from

Two lines of descent share one name. The London announcement credits Shaukat's model, but London's 2026 Fajr and Isha follow the Blackburn chart, not his function.

London's unified timetable

  1. Sep 1987 – Aug 1988Naked-eye Mushahadah in Blackburn (53.45°N), under Hizbul Ulama
  2. 2 Apr 1988 · 2 Jan 1989Ulama agreements: observation over degree times; a chart goes to UK masjids
  3. Oct 2005Miftahi, Fajar and Isha: Tables 5 and 6, the UK interval chart
  4. late 2006Shaukat computes Hizbul Ulama's UK city tables, with his own function and no 1/7 rule; London's timetable is a different table
  5. 1 Aug 2011London adopts the unified timetable inside the M25 (Unified.pdf)
  6. from Jan 2015Chart intervals on HMNAO sun times; sunrise − 3
  7. todayEast London Mosque timetable → londonprayertimes.com API
  8. appAthan: London's default, unchanged under the owner's plan

The endpoint's method values match the site's calculator menu, unchanged since 2011: 0 Hanafi, Shafaq General (listed first); 1 Hanafi, Shafaq Abyad; 2 Shafi'i, Maliki, Hanbali (Ahmer); 3 Ja'fari. For London on 21 June 2026, Isha changes with method (22:42, 23:52 and 22:41), and method 2 puts the Shafi'i time in asr.

adhan 4.4.6's MoonsightingCommittee matches the endpoint to ±1 minute on five of six times up to 64°N. It departs in two ways. It tests the 55° threshold on signed latitude, so 1/7 never applies south of −55°. And it returns Invalid Date in polar night, where the tables print 18° times.

Above 55°: nine written versions

Dated by archived page captures, as "capture (the page's own Updated line)". The published tables follow none of them exactly.

  1. 1998 – 2003prayer.html: fixed 15°, "a value adopted by ISNA"; 1/7 of the night above 45°.
  2. 2003 – 200518° with the 1/7 rule; from October 2004 "a combination of 18 degrees, 15 degrees, and even 12 degrees".
  3. May 2005The latitude-season function arrives; 1/7 removed; near the Arctic Circle, "nearby lower latitudes".
  4. Sep 20091/7 of the night returns, "between 55 - 66 degrees".
  5. May 20111/7 at 55–65°; above 65°, "nearest lower latitudes where the sun sets and rises". Booklet §10 uses this wording.
  6. Oct 2013how-we.html: pure 18° up to 30°, the 18° comparison from 30° to 55°, 1/7 at 55–60°, and a slide to 60° only "If day length is more than 18 hours or less than 6 hours".
  7. Jul 2016The 18° comparison from the equator to 55°; the slide to 60° made unconditional; Fajr redefined as "Tabayyun".
  8. Jul 2017 → liveAbove 60°: 1/7 at 60° "in summer", the functions "in winter".
  9. by Aug 2020 → livefaq_pt.html: Aqrabul-Bilaad, "decreasing the latitude by 0.1 degrees … until a latitude is reached where the sun sets". This contradicts how-we.html beside it.
  10. the tables1/7 of the night at the true latitude, no slide; polar night gives 18° Fajr and Isha with Sunrise and Maghrib dashed; midnight sun dashes all four. The slide to 60° makes the fit worse: Reykjavik matches 1/7 at its own latitude on 365 of 365 days.

The booklet mixes page versions. §12 pairs the "equator to 55°" comparison (first captured July 2016) with the 18-hour day-length test (last captured October 2013). Whether that combination ever stood on the page is being checked now against every archived capture. being dated

Shafaq General is described both ways. Since May 2019 how-we.html says "Shafaq General uses Shafaq Abyad in Summer and Shafaq Ahmer in Winter", the reverse of the sentence before it. The coefficients settle it: General is Ahmer-like in summer and Abyad-like in winter.

The endpoint, as a contract

Measured on 14 September 2026. moonsighting.ahmedbukhamsin.sa/time_json.php equals the site's praytable.php cell for cell: London 6,570 of 6,570, Sydney 2,190 of 2,190.

  • Hosts. www.moonsighting.com/time_json.php: HTTP 500 with an empty body for every parameterised request. The bukhamsin host: 200 JSON, behind Cloudflare, with no terms of use and no Wayback history. The API moved there on 2024-10-31.
  • Shape. A full year only (about 141 KB), no day or month query. Each day is "Jan 01 Thu", with no year. Times are padded with trailing spaces. The field is maghrib.
  • Errors arrive as HTTP 200. Omitting both or time puts a PHP warning before the JSON. A bad tz or method gives a PHP fatal error in the body. lat=95 is accepted.
  • High latitude. ----- for undefined times; evening values after midnight with no day marker. At Longyearbyen the 18 April Fajr prints as 23:36. McMurdo prints Fajr equal to Isha for days. cause unverified

On those days all six times are an hour off. Each date's offset behaves as if sampled between 00:00 and 01:00 UTC the next day, so zones that change later, such as London and New York, come out right: 18 of the 26 zones in the wider sweep are correct on every day. After 20 September 2026, Casablanca follows an older tz rule (103 days at +1). Palestine was not measured. lead-verified

The site itself

Every live path (1,186), 50 archived captures, and every page's hidden comments, scripts and attribute text, each read in full or as a full diff.

  • Seven pages carry the method: how-we, faq_pt, 3books, ever-wonder and the archived prayer, faq_ps and prayer-french pages. None states the coefficients. None mentions London's unified timetable or names a UK mosque that uses the times.
  • The booklet was on offer, 2012 to 2017. "These algorithms are included in the booklet … a hard (xeroxed) copy of the manuscript can be sent by postage mail". It was removed from how-we by July 2016 and from faq_pt by November 2017. That the 2015 booklet is this manuscript fits, but is unverified
  • Dormant since spring 2024. The last sighting reports are dated 7–10 April 2024. The newest "Updated" line is 3 April 2024. The last paid-link note reads "Paid up to 5/8/2024". The calculator's Apple MapKit tokens expired on 2023-08-01. Since 2025 the site sits behind a Cloudflare bot check.
  • The Islamic day. The site's calendars say dates "BEGIN AT SUNSET THE PRECEDING EVENING"; a hosted guest article dissents.
  • Documents (59 of 59). Shaukat computed Hizbul Ulama's 2007 UK directory. Its 2006 and 2009 tables are identical, apply no 1/7 rule even at Aberdeen, and carry errors: Belfast is computed at a point in Essex.

Questions for the owner

Decisions the research can inform but not make. Numbered as in section 5 of the findings.

  1. The source for v2.0. Accept a dependency on the developer's host, on the site's HTML table, or ask Khalid Shaukat or Ahmed Bu-khamsin for a supported endpoint?
  2. The day-early clock change. Show those days as given, treat them as unreadable, report and wait, or allow a narrow, documented correction?
  3. Days with some fields missing. Should a derived time (Duha, Istijaba, night times) show --:-- whenever its base is dashed?
  4. Which method a v2.0 user gets. A default among 0–3, or the user's choice? The site lists Shafaq General first but describes it both ways.
  5. London under v2.0. Confirm the API as London's default, with moonsighting.com as the second option. No London delta can be applied without synthesising times.
  6. Which rule above 55° is the method: one of the nine texts, or the published tables?
  7. adhan. A cross-check, an offline fallback, or not used? The never-synthesise rule suggests not as a source of shown times.
  8. Which Asr London users see. Keep asr (Shafi'i), switch to asr_2 (Hanafi), or offer both? Fix the comments either way?
  9. Presenting the option in London. Say plainly that it is a different timetable from the user's mosque?
  10. Reconstructing London locally. Ruled out, or allowed as an offline check only?
  11. Asking about the edits. Contact East London Mosque or Hizbul Ulama about the 21 edited slots and the 2022 coordinate change?
